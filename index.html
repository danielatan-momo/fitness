Programme Overview

3 weights sessions + 3 rest/active days · Phase 1 (Oct): cycling optional Thursday · Phase 2 (Nov+): cycling mandatory Thursday. Train 10am fasted. All weights at home.

⚠️
Sciatica protocol — always active: No conventional deadlifts. No good mornings. No sit-ups or crunches. No twisting under load. No leg press with low foot placement. Stop any exercise immediately if radiating leg or glute pain occurs. Right side gets extra attention in all core work.
🍷
On wine: Built in at 1–2 glasses, 2–3 nights/week. Red preferred (Pinot Noir, Merlot — lower sugar). Never on an empty stomach. Never the night before a weights session if you can help it. This will slow fat loss by approximately 20–30% compared to no alcohol. That's your call — but you should know the cost.

RPE Guide

All working sets target RPE 8 — you could do 2 more reps if you had to, but it would be hard. Progress weight when any set drops to RPE 6–7.

RPE 5–6Too easy → increase weight
RPE 7One more week then increase
RPE 8 ✓Target — 2 reps in reserve
RPE 9–10Too heavy → reduce load

Phase Overview

Phase 1 · October
Strict keto + IF. Fat adaptation. Foundation strength — master the movements. Lower weight, strict form. Reset metabolism. Expect low energy Week 1–2 (keto flu).
Phase 2 · November
Low carb maintained. Progressive overload begins in earnest. Weights increase weekly. Energy better. Body composition visibly changing. Introduce harder variations.
Phase 3 · December
Peak strength phase. Hold leanness through festive season. Maintain habits under social pressure. Week 13 deload before Christmas. Assess and plan next cycle.

🚴
Why Zone 2: On keto/low carb, Zone 2 (conversational pace, 60–70% max HR) maximises fat oxidation — you're burning fat as fuel, not glycogen. Going harder pushes you into Zone 3–4 where you need carbs you don't have. Zone 2 also builds mitochondrial density and aerobic base without cortisol spike.
⚡
Phase 1 (Oct) — Optional active recovery: With keto adaptation + 3 strength sessions + work, forcing a Thursday ride adds too much cumulative fatigue. If you feel good, go. If not — skip without guilt. Active rest (walk + yoga) is the alternative. Forcing tired rides raises cortisol and stalls fat loss.

Phase 1 — October (Optional)

When to go: Energy feels stable, legs not sore from Monday
When to skip: Fatigued, DOMS lingering, work week was heavy
If you ride: 35–45 min max, Zone 2 only, full conversation pace
Electrolytes: Essential before and during — keto depletes sodium fast

Phase 2–3 — Nov/Dec (Mandatory Thursday)

Duration: 60–75 min
Intensity: Zone 2 base + one 5-min Zone 3 push mid-ride
Progression: Add 1× longer ride (90 min) per month on Sat/Sun ad-hoc
Core focus: Deliberate core engagement on the bike — tight abs, stable pelvis

Active Rest — Tuesday & Saturday

Walk + Yoga · Recovery without full rest · Parasympathetic nervous system activation

Wine on keto: Dry red or white only — no sweet wine, no champagne, no cocktails. 1–2 glasses maximum. Wine does not kick you out of ketosis (minimal carbs) but alcohol IS metabolised before fat, which pauses fat burning for 2–4 hours. Drink with dinner, not before. Creatine monohydrate YOU HAVE Strongest supplement evidence for perimenopause — supports muscle protein synthesis, bone density, and cognitive clarity. 3–5g daily consistently. Safe and cheap. 3–5g any time daily. Consistent timing matters more than when.
Magnesium glycinate YOU HAVE Sleep quality improvement + reduction in nocturia (night waking to urinate) in perimenopausal women. Glycinate form is best absorbed. Not oxide. 300–400mg 30–60 min before bed. Always with food or herbal tea.
Electrolytes YOU HAVE Critical on keto. Sodium, potassium, and magnesium all deplete rapidly when carbs are low. Keto flu is largely an electrolyte deficiency. Essential every day in October. During training session + one other time daily (morning or afternoon). Not at night.
Vitamin B Complex YOU HAVE Energy metabolism — B2, B6, B12 especially important for perimenopause. Supports cognitive function and reduces fatigue. With first meal (post-workout break fast ~11:30am). B vitamins are water-soluble — take with food.
Vitamin C YOU HAVE Immune support + cortisol regulation post-training. Antioxidant — reduces exercise-induced inflammation. 500–1000mg is the useful range. 500–1000mg with first meal. Not immediately before training (may blunt some adaptation signals).
Vaginal probiotic YOU HAVE Oestrogen decline directly affects vaginal microbiome. Keep taking as directed — this is a smart and often overlooked perimenopause supplement. As directed on product. Usually before bed.
Omega-3 (EPA+DHA) ADD Anti-inflammatory, reduces LDL cholesterol, supports brain clarity ("clearer mind" is your goal), reduces exercise-induced soreness. High priority if fatty fish is under 3× per week. 2g EPA+DHA with a meal — ideally dinner. Fish oil burps: freeze the capsule before taking.
Vitamin D3 + K2 ADD Oestrogen decline accelerates bone density loss — this is your most important long-term protection. D3 supports calcium absorption; K2 directs calcium to bones not arteries. Singapore sun is often not enough. 2000–4000 IU D3 + 100–200mcg K2 with a fat-containing meal (fat-soluble). Get D levels checked by GP.
Psyllium husk ADD · FOOD Soluble fibre — binds LDL in the gut before absorption. Can reduce LDL by 5–10% alone. Also helps gut motility which can slow on keto. 1 tbsp in 300ml water with first meal. Drink immediately + follow with another full glass. Every day.
🩺
GP visit — strongly recommended: Nocturia disrupting sleep is a treatable perimenopause symptom. Have D3 levels checked. Have cholesterol retested after 8 weeks of dietary changes. The conversation about HRT is worth having — recent evidence has significantly revised the risk-benefit picture for women in early perimenopause.

Weights & Progression

Starting loads · 12-week targets · Progression rules

⚠️
Equipment limit note: With ~35kg total plates + one barbell, your upper body and single-leg work has plenty of load headroom. Lower body bilateral barbell work (squats, RDL) will approach the equipment limit around Week 6–8. Solution: prioritise unilateral movements (split squats, step-ups, single-leg bridges) which double the effective load, and use tempo (4-sec lowering) to increase difficulty without more weight.

Deload Protocol

Week 5 — Mini Deload
After 4 weeks of keto adaptation + strength foundation. Reduce all weights by 15% for 3 days. Week 4 Friday → Week 5 is lighter. Then reload Week 6.
Week 9 — Full Deload
After 8 weeks continuous training. Reduce all weights by 20% for one full week. Same exercises, strict form focus. Non-optional.
Week 13 — Final Deload
Before Christmas. Light week. Assess all lifts. Set new starting weights for next cycle. Celebrate the 90 days.

Milestones & Expectations

Realistic targets for perimenopause · Monthly check-in metrics

📌
Perimenopause reality: Fat loss is 20–40% slower than at 35. The scale can fluctuate ±2kg week to week from hormonal water retention — this is not fat gain. Body measurements and strength numbers are more reliable progress indicators. A great month looks like: weights up, measurements down, energy better, sleep improving.

Month 1 — October

Keto adaptation · Foundation · Reset

⚡
Energy: Low in Weeks 1–2 (keto flu). Better from Week 3 as fat adaptation settles.
⚖️
Scale: Expect −1.5–2.5kg quickly (water + glycogen). Real fat loss begins Week 3. Don't read into Week 1–2 numbers.
📏
Measurements: Waist may reduce 0.5–1cm from inflammation reduction alone.
💪
Strength: Learn the movements. Focus on form not load. Weights will feel easy — that's correct.
😴
Sleep: Begin nocturia protocol — legs elevated, fluids stop after herbal tea.
🎯
Win condition: 4 sessions/week consistently. Keto strictly followed. That's it.

Month 2 — November

Visible change · Strength building · Energy returns

⚖️
Scale: −2–3.5kg from start. Real fat loss now visible. Scale will stall some weeks — normal.
📏
Waist: −1–2cm from start. Clothes noticeably looser around the waist.
💪
Strength: Meaningfully stronger on all lifts. Bulgarian split squat introduced and progressing. Lat pulldown above 22kg.
⚡
Energy: Noticeably better than October. Clearer mind. Less afternoon slump.
🚴
Cycling: Faster and more powerful on the bike — strength training carry-over is real.
🩸
Cholesterol: Get a retest. 8 weeks of dietary changes should show measurable LDL reduction.

Month 3 — December

Peak strength · Leanest yet · Hold through festive

⚖️
Scale: −3–4.5kg from start (54.5–56kg). Perimenopause makes 5kg in 90 days very difficult — this is honest.
📏
Body fat: ~23–24% (from 27%). 20% is a 6-month target, not 90-day.
📏
Waist: −2–4cm total from start. Hip and thigh definition visible.
💪
Strength: Barbell RDL at 30–35kg. Lat pulldown 28–32kg. Split squat 14–16kg. Measurably strong.
🦴
Long-term: Bone density protecting. The most important invisible result of 3 months of heavy lifting.
🎯
Festive: One higher-carb social meal per week — not the whole week. IF maintained. Don't compensate with extra training.

Beyond 90 Days

What the foundation makes possible

🎯
20% body fat: Achievable at 4–6 months with consistent training + diet. Not 90 days — be honest with yourself on the timeline.
💪
5kg target: Achievable at 4–5 months. The 90-day plan gets you 3–4kg of it.
🔄
Next cycle: Repeat the programme at higher starting weights. The second cycle produces faster results than the first.
🩺
GP visit: You now have 3 months of real data. The conversation about perimenopause management and HRT is worth having with numbers in hand.
🚴
Cycling goal: Add structured cycling training in cycle 2 — longer rides, one interval session per week. The strength base you've built will make this significantly more effective.

Back Health & Sciatica Protocol

20–25 min · Any rest day · 3–4× per week is better than once done perfectly

🦴
Treat this as physio, not a workout. Right side gets extra time throughout — right piriformis tightness compresses the sciatic nerve on your problem side. Do this on Tuesday and Saturday at minimum. It can follow yoga or be standalone.
